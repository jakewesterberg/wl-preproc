## What was built

**BMD is the sixth registered detector** (Mihali, van Opheusden & Ma 2017).
It is a hidden semi-Markov model of fixation: the eye drifts or makes a
microsaccade, and within a state its velocity is constant.
- A Metropolis–Hastings chain samples the state sequence.
- The motor noise, measurement noise and speed distributions are
  re-estimated between rounds of sampling.
- A sample is a microsaccade when the chain puts it in that state in at least
  half of the last round's samples.

**Three modules.**
- `bmd_rng.py` reproduces libc++'s `mt19937_64` and the five distributions
  the reference draws from, because a chain follows its random stream
  exactly or not at all.
- `bmd_table.py` computes the likelihood's table of log A(α, d) from its
  formula, on the reference's grid, with cutoffs chosen by the paper's own
  criterion. Nothing of the authors' table is shipped.
- `bmd.py` holds the smoother, the likelihood, the sampler, the grid search
  (numba, in parallel over samples) and `detect_bmd`.

**In the pipeline, by the requester's decisions of 2026-09-27:**
- **Large saccades come from Engbert–Kliegl.** BMD's model knows only drift
  and microsaccades. `detect_bmd` runs Engbert–Kliegl with its own `gate_*`
  settings, stores those saccades as BMD's `saccade`, and analyses only the
  fixation stretches between them. Its vocabulary is therefore
  `{saccade, microsaccade, drift}`.
- **Settings are pooled per 1-minute block.** Stretches go into blocks of at
  least 60 s, in time order. Each block shares one set of settings, and
  each stretch keeps its own chain. Block b runs with seed `seed + b`.
- **Microsaccades are measured from take-off.** BMD's state-1 run
  `[t1, t2)` carries the eye from sample `t1 − 1`, so a BMD microsaccade on
  an eye's own trace is measured over `[start − 1, stop)`.
  - The rule is declared on the registry entry, as
    `runs_start_after_takeoff`.
  - It is not applied to the conjunction or to BMD's saccade rows, which are
    Engbert–Kliegl's.

**The reference, for tests only.** `github.com/basvanopheusden/BMD` at
`1fab635` has no licence file; the requester has the authors' permission to
use it for testing. `tests/eye/detect/_bmd_reference.py`:
- reads the authors' checkout by path;
- copies `bmd.cpp`/`bmd.h` into a build directory inside it;
- patches the copies by regular expression:
  - the seed comes from the environment, not the clock;
  - the rates and caps become compile-time constants;
  - the two arrays read past their ends get one zero on each side;
  - the settings' `long double` becomes `double`;
- builds them with clang against libc++.

CI installs clang, libc++ and Boost, and clones the pinned commit. Nothing of
the authors' is committed.

## What was measured

- **Fidelity, exact.** Every change point of every sample, and the settings
  at every iteration, match:
  - the authors' own stored example: all six iterations and all 240 samples;
  - the patched reference on 1 kHz simulations (two seeds);
  - 500 Hz simulations with the speed caps (two seeds);
  - a trace with exactly repeated positions;
  - a 5,000-sample stretch of each eye of the reference recording, at the
    rig's 498.55 Hz.

  Two nulls fail the check: chains seeded with the run seed itself, and the
  speed settings started from the run's generator.
- **This implementation's table against the authors'.** Median
  |Δ log A| is 3.4e-7. Printed the way the authors printed theirs, 99.999% of
  the cells BMD reads are identical, and the cutoffs are the same grid
  points. One cell in the authors' file is wrong by 0.0103, and quadrature
  agrees with this table there. With this table in place of theirs, BMD's
  probabilities are identical on the authors' example and on a lab stretch.
- **The reference recording** (uncalibrated, p99→15° scale):

  | | Runtime, full recording | Microsaccades per second (first 120,000 samples) | Microsaccade κ vs Engbert–Kliegl |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Left eye | 261 s, 266 s | 1.35 | 0.569 |
  | Right eye | 280 s, 363 s | 1.43 | 0.569 |

  The two runtimes are the run before the plan and the run during
  execution, on the same machine. The rates and κ were identical both
  times. That is 13 to 18 minutes per eye for a two-hour session.
- **The paper's claim on its own simulated data holds.** At the paper's
  speeds, 1 kHz, motor noise 0.003:

  | Measurement noise | BMD hit rate | Engbert–Kliegl hit rate |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.03 | 0.995 | 0.766 |
  | 0.06 | 0.979 | 0.103 |

- **The planted steps, on a drifting eye.** BMD finds every planted step
  within 5 samples. It makes one detection at no planted step, at sample
  7,456 of 7,800 (15.6 s).

## The requester's decisions, and why they stand

All on 2026-09-27, all in the spec:
- **Use the authors' code as a test reference**, under their permission,
  never shipped (spec preamble, §2).
- **Only fixation stretches are analysed**, and **Engbert–Kliegl's saccades**
  are what is removed (§3.1).
- **Shared settings with a per-stretch search**, pooled per **1-minute
  block** (§3.2).
- **Microsaccades are measured from take-off** (§3.5).
- **Give the test eye real drift, and give BMD its own drifting check.**
  - On the still synthetic eye every other detector is held to, BMD split
    the 0.75° planted step into three and added a spurious one. Its model
    assumes a drifting eye, as every real one is.
  - Measured on standalone traces with 0.03° measurement noise: with no
    drift BMD found exactly the planted step in 3 of 8 cases; with the
    reference recording's drift, in 8 of 8.
  - Giving every session drift broke two existing checks, Otero-Millan's
    microsaccade count and a consensus test on the glissade session. So the
    five existing detectors keep the still sessions, and BMD is held on a
    drifting copy (§5.1).

## The rulings made during execution, and what each costs if wrong

- **Task 2: the lower-cutoff test was strengthened.** The plan said
  mutating `compute_table`'s lower cutoff one grid step up would fail
  `test_the_cutoffs_follow_the_papers_criterion`. It did not. Only the
  gated agreement with the authors' table caught it, because the cutoff test
  checked the upper cutoff's neighbour and not the lower's, though its
  docstring said both.
  - The fix: two assertions pinning the lower cutoff as the last grid alpha
    below the tolerance, or the grid's first. They fail under the mutation
    and pass on the code.
  - Cost if wrong: two assertions too strict for a future change to the
    table's step, which this test would flag at once.

**Notes, not rulings:**
- **Missing Boost gives an unhelpful failure.** With `WLPP_BMD_REFERENCE`
  set but the Boost headers not findable, the build raises
  `CalledProcessError`, and clang's message is captured and hidden. Seen
  while running a mutation check. CI installs Boost, so it cannot happen
  there.
- **One mutation survives, as the plan says** (Task 5). Drawing the
  synthetic drift from the recipe's main random stream instead of its own
  passes every test, because nothing draws from that stream after the drift
  today. The separate stream guards only against a future draw placed after
  it.

## The reference's quirks, kept on purpose

These are what the exact check requires, and every published BMD result was
computed with them (spec §1.10):
- **The table lookup interpolates crosswise, not bilinearly.** The corners
  (α_i, d_j+1) and (α_i+1, d_j) carry each other's weights. Against a true
  bilinear interpolation the difference is median 0.017 in log A and at
  most 1.2, largest at small α; near the drift column (d = 1), at most
  0.0065.
- **Division by zero follows IEEE.** A run whose smoothed displacement is
  exactly 0 divides by zero, and the reference carries on with infinities
  and NaNs. So every compiled function uses numba's numpy error model.
  - This was found on the reference recording's right eye, where the
    tracker repeats a position long enough for the smoother to reach an
    exact fixed point.
- **Two reads past the arrays' ends return 0.** The one before the start
  makes the position before a stretch's first sample the origin. So
  `to_origin` shifts each stretch to put it there, following the paper's
  x₀ = x₁ − 10⁻⁴.
- **`t01[n]` is never matched.** The first and last state-1 runs are forced,
  and are excluded from the probability.

## Two changes to the test build, found while proving the plan

- **The build cache is keyed by the patched sources and the compiler's
  flags.** It was first keyed by the four constants alone, which would reuse
  a stale binary after a patch is edited.
- **`long double` is patched to `double`**, in place of clang's
  `-mlong-double-64`. On x86-64 that flag changes the compiler's type, but
  glibc's `logl`, `powl` and `lgammal` are built for the 80-bit one. This was
  reasoned, not observed; the patch avoids the question, and it was then
  proven on x86-64 Ubuntu.

## Findings

- **Otero-Millan and a drifting eye** (spec §8 item 7). On a synthetic
  session with the reference recording's drift, Otero-Millan reported about
  40 microsaccades on one eye. On pure synthetic drift it reported none, and
  on the real recording its microsaccade rate is within its band. Why is
  unmeasured.
- **The authors' table has one wrong cell in use**, (523, 612), off by
  0.0103. This implementation computes its own table, so it is unaffected.

## What has NOT been measured

- **Whether the model fits monkey data.** An exact reimplementation cannot
  show that. BMD's rows stay provisional until a calibrated session exists
  (parent spec §3.2).
- **Hit and false-alarm rates on real data.** No ground truth exists; the
  κ against Engbert–Kliegl is agreement, not accuracy.
- **Runtime on the compute machine**, which is not assembled.

## Deferred, and NOT done

The open questions in spec §8:
- **Item 6: the crosswise interpolation.** Should a correctly interpolating
  parameter set exist beside the faithful one?
- **Item 7: Otero-Millan's drift finding** (above).
- **Item 8: BMD's one-sample conjunction rows.** BMD's conjunction gets the
  one-sample floor, now shared by five detectors. On an earlier drifting
  run one BMD conjunction microsaccade was one sample long, stored at 0.0°.
- **Item 9: small steps can still split on a drifting eye.** On one
  drifting seed, the 0.75° step came back as three detections.
